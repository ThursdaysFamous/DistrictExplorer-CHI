New York City has 59 community districts, each served by a community board. The board advises on land use, the city budget, and local services — and it is the body most likely to be deciding something about your block this month.

What the lookup shows

Select any point in the five boroughs and the community district card names the district that covers it and the community board that serves it, with the board office's address and contact details where the city publishes them.

Community districts are numbered within each borough — Manhattan CD 7 and Brooklyn CD 7 are different places — so the card names the borough alongside the number.

Community board vs. Council district

A community board is appointed and advisory; a City Council district elects a member who votes. The boundaries are drawn separately and rarely match, so most addresses sit in one of each with different numbers. Toggle both layers to see them together.

Where the boundaries come from

District boundaries and board contacts come from the city's own published data; the map names no body it cannot cite. It is free, requires no login, and sets no tracking cookies.

Not for legal or official use. Boundary and roster data come from public sources that explicitly disclaim legal precision. Confirm district assignments with the relevant government office before relying on them for anything official.
