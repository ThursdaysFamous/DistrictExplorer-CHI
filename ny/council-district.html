New York City has 51 City Council districts, each represented by a Council Member. Your Council district is not your community district, your borough, or your ZIP code — it is its own boundary, and the one that decides who votes on the city budget and land use on your block.

What the lookup shows

Select any point in the five boroughs and the Council card shows the district number that covers it and the Council Member who holds the seat, with their office and contact details where the city publishes them.

Every other district covering the same point is listed beside it — your community district and board, NYPD precinct, school district, State Senate and Assembly districts, and U.S. House district.

Council district vs. community district

They are different systems and they rarely line up. A Council district elects a member of the City Council. A community district is served by an appointed community board that advises on land use, the budget, and local services. One address sits in exactly one of each, and the map shows both at once when you toggle both layers.

Where the boundaries come from

District boundaries and the member roster come from the city's own published data; the map names no officeholder it cannot cite. It is free, requires no login, and sets no tracking cookies.

Not for legal or official use. Boundary and roster data come from public sources that explicitly disclaim legal precision. Confirm district assignments and officeholders with the relevant government office before relying on them for anything official.
