# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## What this is

Chicago District Explorer: a single-file, dependency-light web app. Click a point in Chicago (or search an address) and it reports every civic district containing that point and who represents you there — wards, county/state/federal legislative districts, police districts/beats, school zones, and more. Layers are location-aware: city layers hide outside Chicago, county layers appear only in the counties that publish them, and the statewide layers (county, township, municipality, school district, ZIP) work anywhere in Illinois. Deployed as a static site to `chidistricts.com` (see `CNAME`).

**Coverage is no longer "the Chicago collar."** It began as the seven metro counties and grew one contiguous county at a time across central and northern Illinois — north to the Wisconsin line, west to the Mississippi, and south through the Metro East to the Missouri border (**72 counties served as of 2026-08-21 — 61 through their own dispatch entries, 3 through a shipped judicial circuit, and 8 through the County card alone**; these numbers move every tranche, so read `DISPATCH_COUNTY_FIPS` and `METRO_COUNTY_FIPS` in `scripts/build_metro_outline.py` as the authority, never this sentence). Jo Daviess, the 63rd, is the first county whose boundary was **PURCHASED**: its GIS office sells data under a signed licence, and the board-district shapefile ships displayed under the county's written permission (licence #008382 + the IT/GIS Director's authorization e-mail), with the raw file retained offline rather than committed — `scripts/build_jodaviess_board_districts.py` records the whole arrangement and the component hashes. COLES, the 64th, retired a gap that was never a block: its site answers HTTP 200 and merely serves an **incomplete TLS chain** (leaf only, no intermediate), which every automated client reports as a failure and no browser notices — so "unreachable" had been recorded where "hand me the issuer" was the whole story, and the scraper now supplies that intermediate by AIA with a pinned hash rather than by disabling verification. Coles also carries the fleet's counter-example to the roster-rides-the-boundary shape: its board layer publishes an `Official` column that is a 2022 snapshot and gets **six of twelve names wrong**, so the app reads geometry from the county's service and people from the county's board page (`scripts/coles_county_board_scraper.py`). CLARK, the 65th, is the first county in the fleet built **entirely out of election returns, with no map at any stage**: its Clerk answered the standing ask with "The County Board is elected by districts. I do not have maps available", which settles the board's form and refuses the geometry in one sentence — but the districts are unions of WHOLE PRECINCTS, so the county's own certified canvasses describe them completely and the Census 2020 voting districts (matching its 23 precinct names 23/23, summing to its exact population) supply the polygons; the roster is those same canvasses' certified winners, so every card row names the election that seated the member rather than claiming a currency the returns cannot show (`scripts/build_clark_boundaries.py`, `scripts/clark_county_board_scraper.py`, and `docs/EXPANSION_GUIDE.md` §2.5.1's canvass route — whose step 4, THE JASPER TEST, is the whole difference between Clark shipping and Jasper still not). That build then found the route is not one county's trick: `il-<county>.pollresults.net` / `.accessliberty.com` is one election-results vendor serving **34 Illinois counties**, publishing each one's certified results as structured data, and sweeping its twelve unserved counties the same day shipped three more — CRAWFORD and MERCER as dissolves of their census voting districts (each walking past a standing obstacle: Crawford's layers wait on a county Mapping Committee, Mercer's only map is a 2021 scan), and MOULTRIE straight onto the County card, its board proven AT LARGE by its own returns. The other nine did not ship and that is the sweep's real yield: each now carries a MEASURED blocker instead of a guess, including both enclaves — Bureau splits sixteen precincts between districts, which closes its last route, and Christian re-precincted after 2020. Twelve counties, nine honest no's, and not one e-mail sent; EDGAR followed the same evening as the fourth, and is the first the route has carried to Clark's full two-witnesses-per-district standard — its Clerk's 2022 General canvass covers all seven districts, with 2024 and the 2026 primary re-tabulating disjoint halves. It also settles where a roster comes from: its canvasses elected a District 6 member who has since left, and only the county's own page names the appointee, so GEOMETRY COMES FROM WHATEVER PROVES THE LINES AND PEOPLE FROM WHATEVER THE COUNTY MAINTAINS AS PEOPLE (`docs/DATA_LAYER_GUIDEBOOK.md`, the two 2026-08-18 entries). FRANKLIN, the 70th, proves the route is not one vendor's either: its Clerk publishes on **platinumelectionresults.com**, a THIRD results vendor carrying eight Illinois counties — two of them still unserved (Marion, Wayne) — and its per-precinct race pages compose the board's three districts from three certified elections that agree exactly, the 2026 primary naming the districts outright where the two generals name only candidates. Two traps are recorded with it: the vendor's precinct ids group in a way that reads like a district key and is not one (Browning 1 and 3 sit in District 1, Browning 2 in District 2), and the county's members page is a GRID whose roles sit in the column BEFORE their own row's name, so a flattened read pairs every chairman with the wrong member (`scripts/build_franklin_boundaries.py`, `scripts/franklin_county_board_scraper.py`). That vendor then paid for itself twice more the same afternoon: sweeping its three UNSERVED counties shipped CLINTON as the 71st — five districts whose composition three certified elections agree on, and the tightest population spread this route has produced at 7.3% — and produced two honest no's that are worth as much. MARION is composed completely by its own returns and still cannot be built: five census voting districts under the base names CENTRALIA and SALEM span three board districts each, so no name can place them, where Clinton's one unnameable merge sits wholly inside a single district and therefore cannot move a line. WAYNE had its last open question answered — its certified 2026 primary carries a numbered District 7 contest on Fairfield 1 and Fairfield 2's own ballots — and was still not built, because the composition it confirms runs 1,863 to 3,060 people against a 2,311 ideal, a 32.4% worst deviation beyond the 30% ceiling this project's own dissolve guard enforces (`scripts/build_clinton_boundaries.py`). **Contiguity was retired as a shipping gate on 2026-08-04**: a county now joins whenever a county-keyed layer answers in it, wherever it sits — the served area CAN be disjoint and has been three times (Effingham shipped the same day as that rule, making `metro-outline.json` a MultiPolygon; ZERO islands as of 2026-08-17 evening — Wabash grew the Edwards island to two counties that morning and White, bordering Hamilton on the mainland and both island counties, merged it back in hours later, so the file is a single Polygon again. Islands can un-island and the dissolve is recomputed, never patched: Hamilton was an island for one day until Jefferson, bordering both it and the mainland, merged it back in, Effingham was one for a week until Shelby did the same, and Edwards–Wabash eleven days until White), and each detached county follows the first-island checklist (`docs/EXPANSION_GUIDE.md` §2.5.1). **The mainland also carries exactly three HOLES — ENCLAVES, the inverse of islands: Bureau County (17011), unserved and entirely enclosed by seven served neighbours (Henry, LaSalle, Lee, Marshall, Putnam, Stark, Whiteside); Christian County (17021), enclosed by four (Sangamon, Macon, Shelby, Montgomery) the day Shelby shipped; and KNOX County (17095), enclosed by seven (Henry, Stark, Peoria, Fulton, McDonough, Mercer and Warren) the day Warren shipped — so `metro-outline.json` has FOUR rings in its one polygon.** Menard was an enclave until it shipped on 2026-08-07, and the same join that merged the Effingham island created the Christian enclave on 2026-08-11 — the topology moves in both directions at once. Bureau, Christian and Knox are the three unserved counties with no unserved neighbour, which makes them the counties whose absence a reader can see as doughnuts in the coverage wash. Read the ring count from `build_metro_outline.py --check`, never from this sentence. The county-keyed honesty test is unchanged (never join for a rich statewide answer; a city never carries its unserved county). **The standing per-county view — service tier, board posture, dispatch entries, open gaps — is `docs/COUNTY_STATUS.md`, GENERATED by `scripts/build_county_status.py` from those lists + index.html's dispatch tables + `coverage-gaps.json`; regenerate after any county change (`--check` in CI fails otherwise, never hand-edit it).** `validate_index.py` fails the merge gate if a county gains a dispatch entry without joining the ring. Adding a county adds **dispatch entries and roster rows, never new layers** (`docs/EXPANSION_GUIDE.md` Part 2) — and some add roster rows ONLY: a county electing its board AT LARGE has no district geometry, so its members ride the County card via `data/app/il-county-commissioners.json` with no dispatch entry, no coverage function and no toggle. Thirteen counties ship that way (Monroe, Randolph, Pike, Brown, Calhoun, Putnam, Schuyler, Hamilton, Edwards, Greene, Morgan, Wabash, Moultrie — Monroe, Randolph and Hamilton carry dispatch entries besides). GREENE, added 2026-08-08, is the first that was ALREADY SERVED before its board arrived: a 7th-Circuit subcircuit already answered there, so it moved from the judicial tier to the County card without changing the ring or any anchor. EDWARDS AND WABASH ARE THE TWO WHOSE ROSTERS ARE NOT SCRAPED: neither has a working website (Edwards has none at all; Wabash's domain carries mail and serves no page), so their commissioners ride `DOCUMENT_ROSTERS` in `scripts/il_county_commissioners_scraper.py` — Edwards's carried from a document its Clerk sent, Wabash's from its Clerk's e-mail of 2026-08-16 (names only: her e-mail lists home addresses, which never ship, and no chairman is marked until she answers which of the three chairs) — and the weekly run prints a NOT RE-READ line per county naming the document and its age rather than pretending either was re-read.

**There is no build step, no framework, and no server-side code.** The entire app — styles, core, and all layer modules — lives inline in `index.html` (~21,000 lines). `sw.js` is the service worker; `data/app/*.json` are runtime-fetched data files. Everything else is data pipeline, scrapers, or CI.

**`sources.html` is the app's one sub-page**, and it is where provenance lives. The footer used to carry a ten-link credit row, which could name the publishers but could never answer the question a reader actually has — *where did THIS answer come from?* The page keeps those credits and adds the **layer matrix**: one row per registered layer naming what it answers, the publisher its boundary comes from (linked), where the names on its card come from, and the ground it answers on. It is **GENERATED** from `metro-worksheet.json`'s `layers[]` — the same list that drives `EXPECT_LAYER_IDS`, `LAYER_AREA_RANK` and the smoke test's layer count — so a layer cannot ship without a row and a row cannot outlive its layer; `layers[].source` is now required for every layer (the generator refuses otherwise). The reciprocal move sent the **Data gaps panel's button to the masthead**: absence and provenance are the same reader's question, and the caveat was invisible at the foot of a long page. Both keys (`sources_page`, `verified_date`) are OPT-IN — a sibling fork that carries neither generates nothing and sees a byte-identical file, which is the rule for anything riding the engine release channel.

## Running & testing

```bash
# Run locally — any static server works; internet needed for live-API layers:
python3 -m http.server 8000    # then open http://localhost:8000/

# Behaviour gate (real Chromium boot via Playwright) — the main test:
npm install playwright@1.56.1 && npx playwright install --with-deps chromium
BASE_URL=http://localhost:8000/ node scripts/smoke_test.mjs   # serve first, then run

# Static gate (run after any data/app regeneration or app edit):
python3 scripts/validate_index.py index.html

# Generated-region gate (Conversion 2): per-fork facts live ONCE in
# metro-worksheet.json; GENERATED:BEGIN/END regions in index.html, sw.js,
# sources.html, validate_index.py, smoke_test.mjs, CLAUDE.md, and README.md are
# emitted from it. NEVER hand-edit a GENERATED region — edit the worksheet and
# regenerate:
pip install -c scripts/requirements.txt jsonschema
python3 scripts/generate_metro_files.py            # regenerate in place
python3 scripts/generate_metro_files.py --check    # the CI drift gate

# Source-freshness gate (checks upstream datasets haven't gone stale):
pip install -c scripts/requirements.txt requests
python3 scripts/validate_sources.py            # add --offline to skip network

# Link gate (checks every URL the cards and rosters actually render):
python3 scripts/validate_card_links.py         # --offline lists the surface only

# Roster retention gate (fails when a field stops being published):
python3 scripts/check_roster_retention.py --base origin/main
```

`smoke_test.mjs` is a single end-to-end script, not a framework — there are no "individual tests" to select. It asserts the app boots, registers all layers, classifies a known downtown point against ground truth (school board 12, IL Supreme Court 1, Board of Review 3), and degrades to an isolated error card when a source fails. `node_modules`/`package.json` are intentionally gitignored — this repo never commits build artifacts.

**Sandboxed environments (Claude Code web) — Leaflet CDN egress:** `index.html` loads Leaflet from `cdnjs.cloudflare.com`. In the Claude Code web/sandbox the headless browser cannot reach that CDN — Chromium doesn't use the agent HTTPS proxy, so the request resets (`ERR_CONNECTION_RESET` → `L is not defined` → the app never boots). This is environmental, **not** a code regression; don't chase it in app code. It's handled automatically: a `SessionStart` hook (`.claude/settings.json`) runs `scripts/vendor_leaflet.sh`, which `curl`s Leaflet (curl *does* go through the proxy) into `scripts/vendor/leaflet/` (gitignored). `smoke_test.mjs` then serves those files same-origin via `page.route`, so the app boots. Production and GitHub Actions CI are untouched — they reach the CDN directly and the vendor dir is absent, so the fallback is skipped. To run the smoke test manually in this env, `bash scripts/vendor_leaflet.sh` first (or just rely on the session-start hook).

`validate_index.py` is the merge gate: it confirms `index.html` passes `node --check`, still registers every layer (a drop in the `registerLayer(` count fails), embeds no dataset inline, that every `data/app/` file is present with the expected feature/roster counts, and that **`sources.html` still carries a matrix row for every registered layer and the app still links to it** — a layer whose provenance row is missing reads to a reader as a layer with no source, and a sources page nothing points at is a page nobody reads.

`validate_sources.py` is the **freshness** gate (complements the merge gate above). It catches the failure mode the roster scrapers can't: a publisher silently superseding a dataset the app hardcodes. Chicago Data Portal (Socrata) datasets are re-published under a **new id** each year — the CPS attendance-boundary layers ship a fresh `…SY####` dataset every school year — so the old id keeps returning stale data with no error. The script carries a manifest of every Socrata dataset id and shapefile provenance URL the app depends on and checks: (1) the manifest still matches index.html (drift guard), (2) each Socrata id still resolves and keeps its expected name, searching the portal catalog for a **newer-year edition** of the year-versioned ones, (3) the three shapefile sources (ilsenateredistricting.com / illinoiscourts.gov / cookcountyboardofreview.com) are reachable and their built `data/app/` files present, (4) the live ArcGIS/TIGERweb endpoints resolve. It **never edits the app** — swapping a dataset id is schema-sensitive — it exits non-zero only on hard FAILs and reports a newer edition as a WARN. `.github/workflows/validate-sources.yml` runs it monthly and opens/updates a single tracking issue on any WARN/FAIL (the job stays green; the issue is the signal), matching the roster pattern of "source changes get a human look before they ship." When a dataset id is swapped in index.html, update the manifest in `validate_sources.py` to match. **A source whose block is permanent and measured carries `"blocked": "<reason>"`** — seven do (Joliet, both McHenry sources, both Kendall sources, Lake board roles, the Chicago BOE early-voting page). For those the check INVERTS: unreachable is reported OK ("unreachable AS EXPECTED", still visible in the report), and becoming REACHABLE again is the WARN, because that is the state a human can act on — it means automation may be able to resume. Before that flag existed the monthly issue reopened with the same seven no-op WARNs every month. **HTTP 202 counts as unreachable**, not reachable: "Accepted" is never a document, and it is what the captcha fronts in front of several county sites return (dekalbcounty.org moved behind one around 2026-07-31 — its board scraper failed outright while this validator went on calling the source reachable, because 202 < 400).

`validate_card_links.py` is the **link** gate, and it watches the surface neither of the other two does: the URL a reader clicks. `validate_sources.py` covers dataset endpoints; nothing covered the `primaryLink` on a card or a roster's `sourceUrl` until Macon's card shipped `maconcountyil.gov` — a hostname with **no DNS record at all** — for three days (the county is at `maconcounty.illinois.gov`). It EXTRACTS its surface rather than carrying a manifest: `url:`/`href=` literals in **every authored HTML page** (index.html and sources.html) plus every http string in `data/app/*.json`, ~1,200 URLs across ~540 hosts, so a new county is covered the day it ships with nothing to update. sources.html joined that list the day it shipped, and had to: it took ~40 authored URLs off index.html — the footer credit row plus one boundary link per layer — which scanning index.html alone would have silently dropped off this gate. Three things make its output readable instead of a monthly wall of other people's outages. (1) **Authored vs published.** A URL this repo chose (index.html, a builder's own `SOURCE_URL`) FAILs when dead — it is ours to fix. A URL carried through a roster (the `url`/`profileUrl` fields: a village's website exactly as its county clerk published it, one of which is literally `yahoo.com`) is capped at WARN, and a mere 403 on one is OK — CDN-fronted village sites refuse datacenter clients as a matter of course and there is nothing to do about it. (2) **`EXPECTED_UNREACHABLE`**, the same inversion as `validate_sources.py`'s `blocked` flag and for the same measured reason: six hosts are recorded as refusing this client (Cloudflare challenges, Akamai denials), refusal reads OK, and REACHABLE-again is the WARN. An entry cannot calcify — it also warns if the app stops citing the host, and the inversion caught its own author within an hour: a host recorded off a 429 answered 200 on the next run, which is why 429 is now treated as rate-limiting (back off, retry, and say the probe may have caused it) rather than as a block. (3) **Runtime-built URLs are not probed.** `url: "…/schoolprofiles/" + schoolId` is a prefix, not an address; cps.edu 404s it bare while every built per-school URL resolves, so concatenated literals are named in the report instead of reported dead. Two lessons are baked in from getting them wrong first: DNS is resolved **once per host with retries** (a flaky parallel lookup reported two live sites as having no DNS record), and a 403 from an egress proxy is not a 403 from a site — in the Claude Code sandbox github.com 403s with the proxy's own JSON body, so never record a host from a local run without checking who answered.

`check_roster_retention.py` is the **retention** gate, and it exists because every other roster guard measures the wrong thing. Each builder floors a COUNT — "Boone seats 12", "Brown seats 7" — and on 2026-08-08 every one of those passed while Brown County's seven published e-mail addresses were one commit from deletion: browncoil.org had switched on Cloudflare's e-mail obfuscation, so `mailto:` became `data-cfemail`, and the parser returned seven members carrying nothing. **Seven rows in, seven rows out, guard satisfied, contact gone.** Thirty of the 43 builders additionally floor a field or two (`MIN_EMAILS`, `MIN_PHONES`), which is better, but each floor is hand-set per county, 13 builders have none, and no floor anywhere covers a field nobody thought to name — a new field ships unguarded by construction. So this gate lives outside the builders: it compares every `data/app` roster against the same file at the change's base and asks whether each field still appears on about as many records as before. The shipped file is the baseline, so a field is protected the moment it first ships, with nothing to configure. It fails on a field that **vanishes** (was on ≥2 records, now none), on one that loses at least half its records AND at least 3 of them, and on a record count halving. It ignores new files, new fields and small drops — a member leaving moves these by ones and twos. **The subtlety that cost the first draft its first test: coverage is measured PER SOURCE, not per file.** Pooled across `il-county-commissioners.json`, Brown's seven vanishing e-mails read as 40 → 33, an 18% dip that passed every threshold; ten counties share that file and each can break alone, so each top-level key is measured on its own (files pooling more than 200 sources, like `municipal-officials.json`, stay file-level, where per-group thresholds would fire on every village that reshuffles a page). A legitimate mass change — a consolidated election emptying a column — is recorded in `ACCEPTED_DROPS` with a reason and a date rather than by loosening a threshold for everyone. Wired into `smoke-test.yml`, which is where roster refreshes land as bot PRs.

## Architecture: stable core + pluggable layer modules

All inside `index.html`, wrapped in one IIFE. The full contract and per-thread build log live in `docs/BUILD_PLAYBOOK_1.md`; `docs/OPTIMIZATION_PLAYBOOK.md` holds measured optimization tasks. `docs/DATA_LAYER_GUIDEBOOK.md` is the fleet-wide layer inventory (concept × metro matrix, recorded drops, parity debts, backlog) — update it in the same change as any layer add/rename/remove; the weekly fleet-status run WARNs on drift. `docs/EXPANSION_GUIDE.md` is the **primary deployment guide for all future expansion** — Part 1 the governance taxonomy and county-expansion invariant (a new county adds dispatch entries and roster rows, never new layers), Part 2 the new-Illinois-county recipe, Part 3 statewide growth, Part 4 the new-metro-fork port (Chicago is the reference implementation; each metro is its own fork — completed port records live in `docs/archive/`), Part 5 the new-concept procedure — consult it before any expansion or new-concept proposal. It supersedes the retired `METRO_EXPANSION` / `STATEWIDE_EXPANSION` / `COUNTY_LAYER_CONSOLIDATION` / `MUNICIPAL_COUNCILS` / `ILLINOIS_LAYER_STANDARDIZATION` docs (pointer stubs remain at the old paths; originals verbatim in `docs/archive/`).

**Core** provides: the Leaflet map, click-to-select + debounced Chicago-bounded Nominatim geocoder, a global `state` object `{selectedPoint, sequence, layersOn, ...}`, the layer registry + result-card framework, selected-boundary highlight, and URL-hash permalinks (`#point=lat,lng&layers=ward,school-board`). A small namespace is exposed as `window.ChiExplorer` for debugging.

**Shared utilities** (reuse these; don't reinvent):
- `sanitize(str)` / render via `textContent` — all external strings must go through one of these. Injecting scraped or API text as HTML is treated as a real security bug here.
- `pointInGeometry(pt, geometry)` — the point-in-polygon test every polygon layer's `query` uses.
- `fetchJSONWithRetry(url, opts, retries)` — the standard data-fetch path (retry + failure isolation).
- `haversineMiles(...)` — for the nearest-N layers, which use straight-line proximity instead of point-in-polygon (police/fire stations via the `registerNearestPointLayer` factory; `school-site` as a bespoke block).

**A layer module** is registered via `registerLayer({ id, group, label, overlay: {load, style}, query(point, seq), render(result) })`. `group` is one of `political | safety | schools | geography`. Overlays lazy-load their boundaries on first toggle and are cached; `query` runs locally against the cached geometry. Families of similar layers are built by factory helpers (`registerPolygonLayer`, `registerSchoolZone`, `registerCpsNetwork`, `registerIlgaChamber`, `registerNearestPointLayer`) — follow the existing factory when adding a sibling. The factories derive each layer's hover-popup identity (`hoverName`) from the same properties its card reads, so the two surfaces can't disagree; a bespoke `registerLayer` block declares `hoverName(feature)` explicitly. Optional contract field `pointOfInterest(result) => {label, address} | null` drops a geocoded map pin (used by the school-zone layers). Optional contract field `coverage(point) => boolean | Promise<boolean>` declares where a layer's data applies (location relevance): outside its coverage the layer *hides* — toggle block, card, map overlay, hover-snapshot row, relationship outlines, and query all suppressed — without touching `state.layersOn`, so `layers=` permalinks survive and the layer reappears when a selected point re-enters coverage; a throwing coverage test fails open (the layer runs as if undeclared). Chicago's city-scoped layers declare `chicagoCoverage` (ERSB tiling), the Cook-scoped layers `cookCountyCoverage` (CCBR tiling), and the Will/DuPage/Lake/Kane/McHenry/Kendall county layers `willCountyCoverage`/`dupageCountyCoverage`/`lakeCountyCoverage`/`kaneCountyCoverage`/`mchenryCountyCoverage`/`kendallCountyCoverage` (pre-built county outlines); layers that declare nothing behave exactly as before the field existed. Cross-county concept layers (`county-board`, `judicial-subcircuit`, `fire-district`, `park-district`, `library-district`, `county-precinct`) register through the fork-level `registerCountyLayer` dispatcher — one toggle holding a per-county entry table, coverage = the OR of its counties' tests; polygon-style entries reuse `polygonCountyEntry`, and adding a county to a shipped concept is a dispatch entry, not a new layer (`docs/EXPANSION_GUIDE.md` Part 2). An entry's coverage can be narrower than its county: `county-precinct`'s Cook entry uses `suburbanCookCoverage` (in Cook AND NOT in Chicago) because city precincts belong to the separate `ward-precinct` layer. Two engine loaders exist purely for metro forks and are unused by Chicago's own layers: `loadArcGISPaged` (transfer-cap paging) and `makeSocrataPointLoader` (point datasets whose coordinates live only in properties) — see `docs/EXPANSION_GUIDE.md` Part 4.

**Two invariants that pervade the code:**

1. **Stale-async guard via `sequence`.** Every point selection bumps `state.sequence`. Async work captures `seq` and bails (`if (seq !== state.sequence) return;`) when a newer point has been selected. Preserve this in any code that awaits between selection and render.

2. **Per-layer failure isolation.** Each result card is independent: a layer whose data source is down shows an error + Retry *inside its own card* and never affects the others. Never let one layer's failure throw out of its `query`/`render` into shared code.

**Result-card content order (fleet convention):** a card leads with the layer name (the card header), then the district identifier, then — wherever a verifiable source exists — the representative(s)/officeholder(s), the office location, contact info (phone/email), and a link to more detail, in that order. Deviations are allowed where the concept demands them (nearest-N lists, layers with no elected officer), but when identity, location, or contact data exists in a layer's source, surface it on the card rather than leaving it in the dataset. New layers and card edits follow this order; known gaps are tracked in `docs/DATA_LAYER_GUIDEBOOK.md`'s backlog.

**Honesty rules (non-negotiable, enforced in review):** officeholder data is never guessed. Where no verifiable roster source exists, cards link to the official body instead of inventing a name. External strings are always sanitized or set via `textContent`.

## Cross-metro engine parity

This app is one of several sibling metro forks (NYC at `ThursdaysFamous/DistrictExplorer-NYC` / nyc.chidistricts.com; SF at `ThursdaysFamous/DistrictExplorer-SF` / sf.chidistricts.com); **Chicago is the reference implementation**. The metro-agnostic engine inside `index.html` is fenced with `/* ==== ENGINE:BEGIN <name> ==== */ … ENGINE:END` markers and must stay **byte-identical across forks**; everything city-specific those blocks reference lives in the `METRO:BEGIN config` block near the top of the script. When editing:

- Don't edit inside an ENGINE fence unless the change will be ported to every sibling fork — and port it as the **actual git diff**, never by re-describing the feature in a prompt (same prompt ≠ same code; that's exactly how the forks drifted before the fences existed).
- Region-agnostic changes land in this repo first; siblings apply the diff verbatim.
- Never inline a city-specific value in an ENGINE block — add a variable to the METRO config block instead.
- Verify with `python3 scripts/check_engine_parity.py index.html` (fence lint; `validate_index.py` also runs it) or `--against <sibling path or URL> --strict` (byte comparison). Parity is maintained **by construction**: the engine ships as a hash-verified release artifact (`release-engine.yml` on `engine-v*` tags) that sibling forks splice in via automated bump PRs. A weekly Chicago-only workflow (`engine-parity.yml`) additionally compares the deployed sites as belt-and-suspenders and opens a tracking issue on drift; siblings do not carry it.
- Full protocol + the known reconciliation backlog: `docs/ENGINE_SYNC.md`.

## Data pipeline

Most layers fetch live public APIs at runtime (Chicago Data Portal / Socrata, CPD ArcGIS, Cook County GIS, Census TIGERweb, Nominatim). Layers with **no public API** ship their data as same-origin files under `data/app/`, fetched on first toggle:

- **Boundary geometry** (`school-board-districts.json`, `il-supreme-court-districts.json`, `ccbr-districts.json`, the pre-built `{congress,il-senate,il-house}-districts.json` from TIGERweb via `scripts/build_legislative_boundaries.py`, and `will-county-outline.json` for the coverage test) — mapshaper-simplified from the full-precision GeoJSON in `data/` (originals in `data/source/raw/`). Regenerate with `scripts/build_embedded_boundaries.py` (rare operator step). Service worker serves these **cache-first** (boundaries change ~once a decade).
- **Officeholder rosters + curated lists** (`il-{senate,house}-members.json`, `congress-roster.json`, `cpd-district-info.json`, `ccpsa-district-councils.json`, `will-county-board-members.json`, `kane-county-board-members.json`, `lake-county-board-roles.json` — regenerated **weekly by CI** from scraper output; `kendall-county-board-members.json` + `mchenry-county-board-members.json` hand-verified (those counties block all automated fetch incl. the Internet Archive's crawler — their weekly workflows attempt an engine ladder and track the block on standing issues); `school-board-members.json` hand-curated; `early-voting-sites.json` hand-transcribed per election). Service worker serves these **network-first** so a returning visitor never gets a stale officeholder. Officeholder sourcing for any NEW county/metro layer is determined and built in the same change that ships the boundary (`docs/EXPANSION_GUIDE.md` Part 2 rule 4).

**Scraper → builder pattern** (each roster has a pair): a `*_scraper.py` produces intermediate JSON, a `build_*.py` writes the `data/app/*.json` file with count guards (it refuses to write if too few records resolve). `scripts/requirements.txt` pins deps; the CPD scraper additionally needs Playwright for a Cloudflare managed-challenge fetch. Builders emit plain JSON via `json.dump` into `data/app/`, and the app renders every external string through `sanitize()`/`textContent` — together that closes the injection surface for scraped text. (The historical `js_string()`-style `</script>` + U+2028/U+2029 escaping guard existed only when data was spliced directly into the HTML; it closed a real injection bug then, and becomes mandatory again only if scraped text is ever spliced into HTML/JS — don't.)

## CI workflows (`.github/workflows/`)

- `smoke-test.yml` — runs the behaviour gate on every PR and push to `main`, plus the static drift gates and **`check_roster_retention.py`**, which compares every roster field against the change's base and fails when one stops being published. It checks out with `fetch-depth: 0` for that reason. This is the gate that would have caught Brown's seven e-mails going silently empty; roster refreshes land here as bot PRs, which is exactly where it needs to fire.
- `update-{ilga,congress,cpd,ccpsa,will-county-board,ccbr,county-clerk}-roster.yml` — weekly (staggered) roster refreshes. Each re-scrapes, rebuilds `data/app/`, runs `validate_index.py`, and — if anything changed — **opens a PR rather than committing to `main`.** Officeholder data always gets a human review before it ships. Match this pattern for any new roster: never auto-commit roster changes to `main`.
- **`BOT_PR_TOKEN` (repo secret) — why every roster PR runs CI.** A PR opened with the default `GITHUB_TOKEN` fires **no** workflows: GitHub suppresses recursive triggers, so the smoke test never ran on a bot PR and the PR page showed zero checks. All 13 PR-opening workflows now check out and open their PR as `BOT_PR_TOKEN` instead. It needs Contents **read/write** (push the `bot/*` branch) + Pull requests **read/write** (open it) on this repo — the same shape as `ENGINE_DISPATCH_TOKEN`. Every reference is `${{ secrets.BOT_PR_TOKEN || github.token }}`, so an absent or expired secret degrades to the old behaviour (a PR with no checks) rather than breaking the refresh — which also means **a silently expired PAT looks exactly like the bug it fixed**: if bot PRs stop showing checks, check the token before anything else. Data is still gated either way, because each workflow runs `validate_index.py` on the rebuilt files before opening the PR; the PAT is what makes that visible on the PR itself.
- `validate-sources.yml` — monthly source-freshness check. Runs `scripts/validate_sources.py` **and `scripts/validate_card_links.py`**, folding both reports into one issue; on any WARN/FAIL (e.g. a Socrata dataset superseded by a newer-year edition, or a shapefile source gone unreachable) it **opens or updates a single tracking issue** rather than editing anything — the job stays green, the issue is the signal. Same "surface for a human, don't auto-apply" convention as the roster PRs. The issue title is matched to update in place, so it stays `Layer source drift detected — review needed` even though it now carries link findings too — renaming it would orphan the open issue.
- `fleet-status.yml` — weekly (Mon 15:00 UTC) fleet aggregator, **Chicago repo only**: `scripts/fleet_status.py` reads `metros.json`, checks every fork's deploy/engine-pin/roster state, diffs each fork's layer roster against `docs/DATA_LAYER_GUIDEBOOK.md`'s coverage map, and diffs each fork's shipped `data/app/coverage-gaps.json` against the guidebook's gaps block. That last one is the **only** gate on sibling gap drift: the guidebook lives here, so a sibling's file is generated here and lands there via a bump PR — it has no local `--check`. It also diffs every fork's `docs/ENGINE_SYNC.md` against CHI's and checks that file's ENGINE block inventory against the fences actually present in `index.html`/`sw.js` — the file that says "the SAME copy ships in every fork" had drifted 164 lines with an inventory naming 50 blocks against 53 real fences, because `check_engine_parity.py` compares fenced CODE and nothing compared the document. Doc drift is reported in BOTH directions: a fork holding lines CHI lacks says "reconcile both ways", never "overwrite". Reports on a single standing issue.
- Engine release machinery — `release-engine.yml` (publishes a hash-verified engine artifact on `engine-v*` tags and fans out bump PRs to the sibling forks), `create-engine-tag.yml` (tag helper), `engine-parity.yml` (weekly deployed-site comparison, belt-and-suspenders; Chicago only).
- `deploy-pages.yml` — publishes `main` to GitHub Pages (chidistricts.com).

## Conventions

- Code style is ES5-flavored (`var`, `function` expressions) throughout `index.html` — match it when editing existing modules.
- The "verified" date shown in the UI lives in `metro-worksheet.json` as `verified_date`; bump it there and regenerate when reverifying data sources. It used to be hand-written near the boot block in `index.html`, which was fine while one surface printed it — `sources.html` prints it too, and two hand-kept copies of one date is how they drift, so both are now GENERATED regions off that one key.
- This is a public-facing civic tool that explicitly disclaims legal precision — accuracy and the honesty rules matter more than feature velocity.
