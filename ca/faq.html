Common questions about San Francisco districts

What district am I in?

Enter your address or ZIP code in the search box, or click your location on the map above, and the tool lists every district that contains that exact point — your Board of Supervisors district, neighborhood, SFPD district, school attendance areas, California Senate and Assembly districts, U.S. House district, and more — along with who represents you in each one.

How do I find my San Francisco supervisor district?

Search your address or click your location on the map. The tool shows your supervisor district and the Supervisor who currently holds the seat, rebuilt weekly from DataSF's own Current Supervisor Districts dataset.

How many supervisor districts does San Francisco have?

Eleven. Each elects one member of the Board of Supervisors, and the app carries all eleven.

What SFPD district am I in?

San Francisco is divided into ten police districts. Select any address above to see which one covers it and how to reach its station.

What San Francisco neighborhood am I in?

The app uses the 41 Analysis Neighborhoods that DataSF publishes — the boundaries the city itself uses for planning and statistics, which are not always the name a listing or a local would use for the same block.

Who represents me in Sacramento and in Congress?

Pick a location to see your California State Senate district, State Assembly district and U.S. House district, each with the member who currently holds the seat.

Who is my BART director?

BART is governed by an elected nine-member Board of Directors, each from a district that crosses city and county lines. Select a point in San Francisco to see which director's district covers it.

Where do I vote early?

The app carries San Francisco's early-voting sites for the current election and shows the ones nearest your point. Confirm hours with the Department of Elections before travelling.

Is this an official government tool?

No. It draws on public data sources that disclaim legal precision, so always confirm district assignments and officeholders with the relevant government office before relying on them for anything official.
